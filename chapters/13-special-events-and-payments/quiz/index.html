    Quiz: Special Events, Payments, and Term Wrap-Up¶

    Test your understanding of special event formats beyond a regular session, payment processing and scholarships, and the policies for weather, makeups, and term-ending celebrations with these review questions.

    1. What is a single-session workshop?¶

    1. A multi-day intensive format run during a school break
    2. A recurring weekly session open only to enrolled members
    3. One standalone session, often open to non-members, focused on a single skill and doubling as informal recruiting
    4. A private meeting between the club leader and a prospective venue
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter defines a single-session workshop as one standalone session, often open to non-members, focused on a single skill, such as a two-hour "build your first LED badge" workshop that doubles as informal recruiting. Option A describes the weeklong intensive format instead. Option B contradicts the workshop's open-to-non-members framing. Option D describes an unrelated venue meeting, not a student-facing event.

    Concept Tested: Single Session Workshop See: Single Session Workshop

    2. What is the goal of a scholarship application process?¶

    1. To require detailed proof of financial need before waiving any fee
    2. To publicly recognize which families received a fee waiver
    3. To replace the need for a parental consent form
    4. To ensure fees never become the reason a student can't attend, through a simple, low-friction form kept as private as possible
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter defines the scholarship application process as a simple, low-friction form -- ideally requiring no proof of need beyond a parent's request -- that waives or reduces a fee while staying private so a scholarship student never feels singled out. Option A directly contradicts the chapter's "no proof of need" recommendation. Option B contradicts the chapter's emphasis on privacy. Option C confuses this with an unrelated Chapter 3 safety document.

    Concept Tested: Scholarship Application Process See: Scholarship Application Process

    3. What is a showcase event?¶

    1. The club's own end-of-term exhibition where students present finished projects to family and community members
    2. A recurring weekly session focused on debugging practice
    3. An external contest requiring its own registration deadlines
    4. A private mentor training session held before the term begins
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter defines a showcase event as the club's own end-of-term exhibition where students present finished projects, described as the single highest-visibility event most clubs run all year. Option B describes a regular curriculum session, not a special exhibition. Option C describes competition event scheduling, a distinct external-contest format. Option D describes mentor onboarding, an unrelated internal process.

    Concept Tested: Showcase Event Planning See: Showcase Event Planning

    4. Why does the chapter recommend a built-in payment processor inside a registration platform rather than handling cash in person for fee-based events?¶

    1. Because cash payments are illegal for programs serving minors
    2. Because it avoids both bookkeeping headaches and safety concerns around minors handling money
    3. Because a built-in processor is always cheaper than any alternative
    4. Because Eventbrite is the only platform that accepts payments
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter states that a built-in payment processor avoids both bookkeeping headaches and safety concerns around minors handling money, which cash handled in person would introduce. Option A invents a legal prohibition on cash the chapter never states. Option C makes an unsupported cost comparison the chapter does not make. Option D overstates Eventbrite's role, since the chapter frames it as one option among several compared in a registration software comparison.

    Concept Tested: Payment Processing For Fees See: Payment Processing For Fees

    5. Why does the chapter say calendar sync for families reduces forgotten sessions more effectively than individual reminders?¶

    1. Because it eliminates the need for a weather cancellation policy
    2. Because it is required for any club that charges fees
    3. Because it lets a family add the club's full schedule to their own phone or household calendar with one click, rather than depending on catching each individual reminder
    4. Because it automatically cancels a session if too few families sync their calendars
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter explains that calendar sync lets a family add the entire schedule at once, reducing the odds of a forgotten session far more effectively than relying on catching each individual reminder message. Option A confuses calendar sync with an unrelated weather policy. Option B invents a fee-based requirement not discussed. Option D fabricates an automatic-cancellation mechanism the chapter never describes.

    Concept Tested: Calendar Sync For Families See: Calendar Sync For Families

    6. Why does the chapter say time zone consideration matters specifically for a virtual guest speaker session?¶

    1. Because virtual guest speakers are only available in the club's local time zone
    2. Because the club's insurance policy does not cover virtual events
    3. Because a registration deadline tied to a specific time needs its time zone stated explicitly, rather than assuming everyone shares the venue's local time
    4. Because time zone differences make video calls illegal for minors
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter explains that a registration deadline or virtual guest speaker session tied to a specific time needs its time zone stated explicitly, rather than assuming everyone shares the venue's local time, since a spread-out or partially remote community may not. Option A invents a locality restriction on guest speakers the chapter never states. Option B fabricates an insurance limitation unrelated to time zones. Option D makes an unsupported and implausible legal claim the chapter never suggests.

    Concept Tested: Time Zone Consideration See: Time Zone Consideration

    7. A club wants to give students direct exposure to a real technology workplace by visiting a local company's office. Which special event format does this describe, and what does it require beyond a typical session?¶

    1. Field trip coordination, which requires its own consent paperwork layered on top of the standard parental consent form
    2. Guest speaker scheduling, which requires no additional paperwork
    3. Open house event, which happens on the club's own premises
    4. Family night event, which involves parents building alongside students
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter defines field trip coordination as taking students off-site to a place like a local tech company, requiring its own consent paperwork layered on top of the standard parental consent form from Chapter 3. Option B describes bringing a speaker to the club, the reverse of an off-site visit. Option C describes an event held at the club's own venue, not an outside location. Option D describes a parent-inclusive session, unrelated to workplace exposure.

    Concept Tested: Field Trip Coordination See: Field Trip Coordination

    8. A club has never written down who decides whether to cancel for snow, by what time, or through which channel. On a stormy morning, families are already texting to ask, and the club leader scrambles to make an inconsistent, rushed call. What does the chapter say should have been in place beforehand?¶

    1. A registration software comparison completed before the term began
    2. A weather cancellation policy stating in advance who makes the call, by what time, and through which channel families are notified
    3. A competition event scheduling document
    4. A time zone consideration statement
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter's warning is precisely this scenario: deciding a cancellation policy mid-storm, with families already texting, produces a rushed and inconsistent call, which is why the weather cancellation policy should be written into the club policies handbook in advance. Option A confuses this with an unrelated platform-selection decision. Option C describes an external contest scheduling matter, unrelated to weather. Option D describes a time-zone clarification, not a weather-related decision process.

    Concept Tested: Weather Cancellation Policy See: Weather Cancellation Policy

    9. After a session is canceled for weather, a club must decide whether that session's content is simply skipped, folded into the next session, or rescheduled as a standalone add-on. Which concept governs this decision, and when should it be made?¶

    1. The scholarship application process, decided the same day as the cancellation
    2. The end of term celebration, decided at the start of the term
    3. The registration software comparison, decided after the cancellation occurs
    4. The makeup session policy, decided ahead of time rather than improvised the day a cancellation happens
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter defines the makeup session policy as deciding in advance what happens to a canceled session's content, rather than improvising the decision on the day it happens. Option A confuses this with an unrelated fee-waiver process. Option B describes a term-ending event, not a response to a specific cancellation. Option C misapplies a platform-comparison decision to an unrelated makeup-scheduling question.

    Concept Tested: Makeup Session Policy See: Makeup Session Policy

    10. A club runs a five-day, all-day intensive on robotics during spring break, compressing what would normally be a full term's worth of one topic into consecutive daily sessions. A different club instead runs a single two-hour "build your first LED badge" session open to the public. How do these two event formats differ in purpose?¶

    1. Both formats serve the exact same purpose and differ only in name
    2. The five-day event is a competition event scheduling format, while the two-hour event is a showcase event
    3. The five-day event is an open house event, while the two-hour event is a field trip
    4. The five-day event is a weeklong intensive format for deep focus on one topic, while the two-hour event is a single-session workshop aimed at low-commitment recruiting
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter's table distinguishes the weeklong intensive format, suited to deep focus on one topic during a school break, from the single-session workshop, suited to recruiting new members through a low-commitment trial. Option A ignores the clear functional distinction the chapter draws between the two formats. Option B misapplies both terms to formats that do not match either scenario. Option C similarly misapplies unrelated event-format labels to these two scenarios.

    Concept Tested: Weeklong Intensive Format See: Weeklong Intensive Format
