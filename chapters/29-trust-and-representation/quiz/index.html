    Quiz: Building Trust and Representation in Your Club¶

    Test your understanding of earning trust with communities a club has not yet reached, curriculum representation, visible role models, and community partnerships with these review questions.

    1. How does culturally responsive teaching differ from representation in curriculum?¶

    1. They are the same concept described with two different names
    2. Culturally responsive teaching is about how a topic gets taught and illustrated, using relevant metaphors and project themes, while representation in curriculum is about whose faces, names, and stories actually appear in the club's material
    3. Representation in curriculum only applies to challenge cards, never to slide decks
    4. Culturally responsive teaching requires translated materials, while representation in curriculum does not
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter explicitly distinguishes the two: culturally responsive teaching is about the metaphors and project themes used to teach a concept, while representation in curriculum is about who gets credited and shown in the club's own material -- a lesson can do one well and still fail the other. Option A ignores this explicit distinction the chapter draws. Option C invents a challenge-card-only restriction not stated in the chapter. Option D confuses representation with translated materials, a separate Chapter 28 concept.

    Concept Tested: Culturally Responsive Teaching See: Culturally Responsive Teaching

    2. What is role model visibility?¶

    1. The number of women who have applied to be a mentor
    2. A written policy stating a club welcomes all genders
    3. A translated version of a club's promotional flyer
    4. The aggregate, lived effect of representation and mentor recruitment combined -- whether students actually see an adult who shares their background leading and demonstrating in front of the group, not just being technically on the roster
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter's worked example shows a female mentor who ran the sign-in table all semester, technically present but never seen leading -- role model visibility means being seen actually doing the work, not just being listed as a mentor. Option A describes recruitment numbers, not the visibility effect itself. Option B describes an abstract policy statement, not lived visibility. Option C describes translated materials, an unrelated language-access concept.

    Concept Tested: Role Model Visibility See: Role Model Visibility

    3. How does an assistive technology option differ from Chapter 28's disability accommodation?¶

    1. Assistive technology options are only available to advanced-track students
    2. They are the same concept, and Chapter 28 simply used a different name for it
    3. An assistive technology option is a specific tool, device, or software feature -- such as a switch-access adapter or text-to-speech -- while disability accommodation covers the physical room and pace
    4. Disability accommodation is always more expensive than an assistive technology option
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter explicitly distinguishes the two: assistive technology is the equipment and software layer, like a switch-access adapter or text-to-speech extension, while disability accommodation from Chapter 28 covers the physical room, ramps, seating, and pace. Option A invents a track restriction not discussed anywhere in the chapter. Option B ignores the chapter's explicit distinction between the two concepts. Option D makes an unsupported cost comparison the chapter never states.

    Concept Tested: Assistive Technology Option See: Assistive Technology Option

    4. Why does the chapter recommend a leader visit a community organization's regular meeting at least twice, asking for nothing, before requesting a room or a referral?¶

    1. Because trust accumulates through consistent, low-pressure presence and follow-through, and a leader who shows up repeatedly without an immediate ask earns more standing than one who arrives once with a polished pitch and a request attached
    2. Because most community organizations require a formal written application before any request
    3. Because a single visit is against most venues' insurance policies
    4. Because community organizations only meet twice per year
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter's own worked comparison shows a cold email going unanswered while a leader who attended three meetings without asking for anything received an unprompted space offer by the fourth visit, illustrating that trust builds through repeated low-stakes presence. Option B invents a formal-application requirement the chapter never mentions. Option C fabricates an insurance restriction unrelated to community trust building. Option D makes an unsupported and implausible claim about meeting frequency.

    Concept Tested: Community Trust Building See: Community Trust Building

    5. Why does the chapter say a single citywide flyer usually reaches almost no one in urban community outreach?¶

    1. Because citywide flyers cost more to print than neighborhood-specific ones
    2. Because cities do not have any community centers or library branches
    3. Because urban outreach is illegal without a city permit
    4. Because a dense city is actually many distinct neighborhoods, each with its own trusted local hub, and urban families trust the hub closest to them rather than "the city" in the abstract
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter explains that a dense city is actually many distinct neighborhoods, each with its own trusted local hub -- a specific recreation center or library branch -- so urban families trust the hub closest to them rather than a generic citywide message. Option A makes an unsupported cost claim unrelated to the actual reasoning given. Option B is factually wrong, since the chapter explicitly names recreation centers and library branches as urban outreach hubs. Option C invents a permit requirement not discussed in the chapter.

    Concept Tested: Urban Community Outreach See: Urban Community Outreach

    6. How does female mentor recruitment differ from recruiting girls and women, the Chapter 28 concept it builds on?¶

    1. They are identical concepts, just used in different chapters
    2. Female mentor recruitment brings women into the club's mentor team specifically, as adults in a professional role, while recruiting girls and women (Chapter 28) targets students joining as participants
    3. Female mentor recruitment only applies to clubs with more than twenty students
    4. Recruiting girls and women requires a signed data sharing agreement, while female mentor recruitment does not
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter explicitly distinguishes female mentor recruitment as bringing women into the mentor team specifically, distinct from recruiting girls as students, since a club can succeed at the latter while still sending an unintended message if every adult running a session is male. Option A ignores the chapter's explicit distinction between the two concepts. Option C invents a club-size restriction the chapter never states. Option D fabricates a data-sharing-agreement requirement unrelated to either recruiting concept.

    Concept Tested: Female Mentor Recruitment See: Female Mentor Recruitment

    7. A club partners with a mosque's community center for free meeting space. The mentor works with the youth coordinator rather than the imam directly, schedules around prayer times, keeps all content secular, and notes on the flyer that the venue is open to all families regardless of faith. What does this illustrate?¶

    1. Bias awareness training
    2. Inclusive marketing materials
    3. A faith-based venue partnership, which works best when a club approaches the education or youth-program coordinator and keeps its own programming secular regardless of which building hosts it
    4. Role model visibility
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's own worked example describes exactly this partnership, emphasizing working with the youth coordinator, scheduling around prayer times, staying secular, and stating openly that the venue welcomes any family. Option A describes mentor self-reflection training, unrelated to a venue partnership. Option B describes flyer and sign-up form design more broadly, not specifically this partnership arrangement. Option D describes visible leadership by underrepresented mentors, unrelated to venue arrangements.

    Concept Tested: Faith Based Venue Partnership See: Faith Based Venue Partnership

    8. A club forming in a farming county struggles with sign-ups through the school newsletter, since many families homeschool or attend schools outside the county. The mentor instead partners with the county's 4-H extension office and sets up a table at the county fair, and sign-ups more than double. Which concept does this illustrate?¶

    1. Assistive technology option
    2. Urban community outreach
    3. Immigrant family outreach
    4. Rural community outreach, which identifies which existing community hub already has a rural family's trust -- such as a county extension office or 4-H chapter -- rather than treating rural families as simply harder to reach
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter's own worked example describes exactly this scenario, showing rural outreach succeeding by plugging into an already-trusted hub like the 4-H extension office rather than relying solely on a school-based channel. Option A describes equipment for students with disabilities, unrelated to outreach channels. Option B describes a fundamentally different context of many distinct city neighborhoods, not a farming county. Option C describes outreach to immigrant-serving organizations, unrelated to this rural scenario.

    Concept Tested: Rural Community Outreach See: Rural Community Outreach

    9. At a regular mentor meeting, the group discusses real scenarios -- who gets called on first, whose mistakes get framed as "still learning" versus "not a good fit" -- to help mentors notice their own unconscious assumptions about who "looks like" a good coder. Which concept does this describe?¶

    1. Equity audit of club
    2. Bias awareness training, which helps mentors notice their own unconscious assumptions so attention and opportunities to lead don't quietly flow toward students who resemble a mentor's own background
    3. Community trust building
    4. Inclusive marketing materials
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter describes bias awareness training as exactly this kind of scenario-based discussion at a mentor meeting, requiring no formal course, aimed at noticing unconscious assumptions about who "looks like" a good coder. Option A describes a structured, scored review of club practices, a different and more formal process. Option C describes earning an outside community's confidence, unrelated to internal mentor reflection. Option D describes flyer and form design, unrelated to a mentor discussion.

    Concept Tested: Bias Awareness Training See: Bias Awareness Training

    10. The chapter's equity audit checklist always names exactly one lowest-scoring category and one specific item within it, rather than displaying a generic "keep improving" message. Why does the chapter design the audit this way?¶

    1. Because a scored, item-level checklist naming one concrete next action gives a leader something specific to fix, rather than leaving them with only an abstract sense of "doing fine" or "needs work"
    2. Because a generic message would violate the club's data privacy policy
    3. Because naming only one category is a technical limitation of the audit tool that could not be fixed
    4. Because every club scores identically on all five categories, so only one needs to be shown
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter's instructional rationale is explicit: naming one concrete next action produces a prioritized to-do item instead of a vague overall impression, matching the chapter's outcome of identifying one concrete next step to pursue. Option B invents a data-privacy connection that has nothing to do with audit design. Option C mischaracterizes a deliberate design choice as an unavoidable technical limitation. Option D makes an unsupported and implausible claim that every club scores identically.

    Concept Tested: Equity Audit Of Club See: Equity Audit Of Club
