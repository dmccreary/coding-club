    Quiz: Establishing an Oversight Board and Inventory System¶

    Test your understanding of building an oversight board and the succession plan it exists to execute, paired with a club-wide inventory system and mentor recruiting at scale with these review questions.

    1. What is an oversight board?¶

    1. A small standing group, separate from the mentors who run weekly sessions, that holds formal authority over a club's mission, budget, and major decisions, and continues to exist as a body even as its members change
    2. A weekly meeting where mentors plan the next session's challenge cards
    3. The founder's personal list of trusted friends and family
    4. A software tool that tracks kit checkout and return
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter defines an oversight board as a standing group separate from session-running mentors that holds formal authority and persists as a body regardless of which specific people currently sit in its seats. Option B describes routine session planning, an unrelated mentor activity. Option C describes an informal, undocumented network, the opposite of a formal governing body. Option D confuses the board with the equipment checkout system, an unrelated inventory tool.

    Concept Tested: Oversight Board See: Oversight Board

    2. Why does the chapter warn against setting every board seat's term to start and end in the same year?¶

    1. Because a board is only legally required to have staggered terms in some states
    2. Because unstaggered terms guarantee all seats expire simultaneously, leaving the club with an entirely inexperienced board overnight -- the exact loss-of-institutional-memory problem term limits were meant to prevent
    3. Because unstaggered terms automatically disqualify a club from grant funding
    4. Because term limits only apply to the treasurer seat, never the other four seats
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter's own worked example describes exactly this risk: a board with every seat's term starting and ending the same year risks all five members reaching their limit simultaneously, leaving an entirely inexperienced board overnight. Option A invents a state-by-state legal requirement the chapter never mentions. Option C fabricates a grant-disqualification consequence unrelated to term staggering. Option D wrongly restricts term limits to one seat, when the chapter applies them to every seat.

    Concept Tested: Board Term Limits See: Board Term Limits

    3. How does a club inventory system extend beyond Chapter 21's per-kit-type documentation sheet?¶

    1. It replaces the need for any documentation sheet entirely
    2. It only applies to laptops, never to hardware kits
    3. It gives every individual physical item its own unique identifier, distinguishing one specific gyroscope kit from an identical one sharing the same kit-type documentation, wiring diagram, and code template
    4. It is identical to Chapter 21's documentation sheet, just renamed
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's own worked example shows the gap this closes: two identical gyroscope kits share one documentation sheet under Chapter 21's system, but when one goes missing, nothing distinguishes which physical kit is unaccounted for until each item gets its own unique identifier. Option A overstates the change; the documentation sheet still matters, now linked per item. Option B invents a laptop-only restriction the chapter never states. Option D ignores the meaningful distinction the chapter draws between kit-type documentation and individual-item tracking.

    Concept Tested: Club Inventory System See: Club Inventory System

    4. A club with 42 individual items prints one sheet of numbered labels running #001 through #050 for about $6. Using the chapter's own worked example, how many spare numbers remain after every current item is tagged?¶

    1. 50
    2. 42
    3. 6
    4. 8
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter's own worked example states exactly this: printing numbers #001 through #050 for 42 current items leaves eight numbers spare for the next few purchases. Option A reports the total sheet size, not the remaining spare count. Option B reports the current item count, not the leftover numbers. Option C reports the dollar cost of the sheet, not the spare number count.

    5. A semester-end audit expects to find 42 logged items but physically counts only 40. Checking the checkout log accounts for one item currently on loan. Following the chapter's own worked example, what happens next?¶

    1. Exactly one item, sticker #019, is genuinely unaccounted for, and the lost item policy's grace period begins for that one item
    2. The audit is declared invalid and must be redone from scratch
    3. All 42 items are immediately marked lost, regardless of the checkout log
    4. The mentor who last checked out any item is automatically billed for a replacement
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter's own worked example resolves this exact scenario: after the checkout log accounts for one item on loan, exactly one item, sticker #019, remains genuinely unaccounted for, triggering the lost item policy's grace period rather than an immediate loss declaration. Option B is an unsupported overreaction the chapter never suggests. Option C ignores the checkout log's ability to account for most of the discrepancy. Option D directly contradicts the chapter's no-blame lost item policy, which does not automatically bill anyone.

    Concept Tested: Inventory Audit Process See: Inventory Audit Process

    6. According to the chapter's mentor recruiting channel comparison, the engineering meetup post generates the most candidates (7) but only a 43% qualification rate, while the founder's personal network generates fewer candidates (3) but a 100% qualification rate. A club needs to fill many open mentor slots quickly and has time to run several trial sessions. Which channel does the chapter's own reasoning favor, and why?¶

    1. The founder's personal network exclusively, since it has the highest qualification rate
    2. The engineering meetup post, since its larger candidate pool produces more qualified mentors in absolute terms even at a lower qualification rate, provided the club budgets time for more trial sessions
    3. Parent referral only, since it also has a 100% qualification rate
    4. No channel should be used until a written succession plan exists
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter's own annotation on the comparison chart flags exactly this tradeoff: the meetup post has the largest reach but the lowest qualification rate, so a club with time for more trial sessions should budget for that larger volume rather than relying only on higher-rate but lower-volume channels. Option A ignores the stated need to fill many slots quickly, which a small personal network cannot satisfy alone. Option C ignores that parent referral's small candidate count (2) cannot fill many open slots either. Option D invents an unrelated succession-plan prerequisite for mentor recruiting.

    7. Club A never formed a board and went on informal hiatus within six weeks of its founder leaving. Club B formed a five-person board eight months before its founder's departure and ran its first session under new leadership twelve days later. Analyze what specifically made the difference between these two outcomes.¶

    1. Club B simply had a more talented founder than Club A
    2. Club B had more students enrolled than Club A
    3. Club B's board had already named an acting lead mentor and documented which signing authorities the chair and treasurer held independent of the founder, executing a written succession plan rather than improvising a response under pressure
    4. Club A's failure was caused by an inadequate inventory system, unrelated to governance
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's own worked example attributes the difference specifically to Club B's prepared succession plan -- a named acting lead and pre-established signing authority -- not to any difference in founder talent, enrollment, or inventory practices. Option A is explicitly not the reason the chapter gives, which focuses on structural preparation, not individual talent. Option B introduces an enrollment comparison the chapter never makes between the two clubs. Option D confuses this governance scenario with an unrelated inventory-system topic from later in the chapter.

    1. The dashed trigger represents a routine, expected event that happens every year on schedule, just like a term limit
    2. There is no meaningful distinction; the dashed line is purely a visual styling choice with no analytical purpose
    3. The dashed line indicates that a founder's departure should be handled by the equipment checkout system instead of the board

    Show Answer

Option B dismisses a deliberate design choice the diagram's own documentation explains has analytical meaning. Option C confuses succession planning with an entirely unrelated inventory-tracking tool.

    9. Two mentor candidates both pass their background check cleanly. One has deep professional experience but grows visibly frustrated when a student needs the same wiring fix explained three times. The other has less technical depth but stays calm and patient through the same repeated question. Evaluate which candidate the chapter's trial-session process would favor, and why a resume and background check alone would not have revealed this.¶

    1. The patient candidate is the better fit despite less technical depth, since interpersonal patience with young students is exactly what a one-session trial reveals and a resume or background check cannot capture
    2. Neither candidate should be selected, since both revealed some flaw during the trial
    3. The professionally experienced candidate should always be preferred, since technical depth matters more than patience in a coding club
    4. The choice should be made by coin flip, since both candidates passed the background check equally
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter's own worked example concludes that the calm, patient candidate is the better fit for physical-computing work with young students, precisely the kind of interpersonal quality only a trial session -- not a resume or background check -- can reveal. Option B misreads the scenario; the chapter treats the patient candidate as a clear, not flawed, fit. Option C privileges technical depth in a way the chapter's own example explicitly argues against for this context. Option D ignores the chapter's whole point that the trial session is specifically what should inform the decision, not chance.

    10. A new club is forming its first five-seat oversight board this year and wants to avoid the unstaggered-term problem the chapter warns about. Design a staggered term schedule for the five seats, each on a two-year term, that ensures no more than two seats ever expire in the same year.¶

    1. All five seats begin and expire together in year two, then again together in year four
    2. Two seats expire at the end of year one, two seats expire at the end of year two, and one seat expires at the end of year three, with terms renewed on staggered two-year cycles from each seat's own starting point thereafter
    3. All five seats are assigned random, unplanned expiration dates decided informally each year
    4. One seat never expires, while the other four all expire simultaneously at the end of year one
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. This mirrors the chapter's own staggering example -- seats expiring across different years rather than together -- so that at least three experienced members remain at any single turnover point, matching the chapter's explicit two-in-year-one, two-in-year-two, one-in-year-three staggering pattern. Option A recreates the exact unstaggered problem the chapter's warning is built to prevent. Option C abandons the deliberate, written scheduling the chapter recommends in favor of informal decisions. Option D creates an uneven, poorly designed structure that still empties most of the board at once, the opposite of effective staggering.
