    Quiz: Growing, Branding, and Promoting Your Club¶

    Test your understanding of crafting a pitch, deciding whether to scale, building a brand, and promoting a club through partnerships, media, and milestone events with these review questions.

    1. What is an elevator pitch?¶

    1. A written grant proposal submitted to a local foundation
    2. A thirty-second spoken summary of what the club does and why it matters
    3. The club's full annual budget presented to an advisory committee
    4. A signed agreement between the club and a venue
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter defines an elevator pitch as a thirty-second spoken summary answering who the club serves, what students do, and why it's different, named for the time it takes an elevator to reach its floor. Option A describes a grant proposal, a written document for funders. Option C describes a budget presentation, unrelated to a spoken pitch. Option D describes a venue agreement, a different legal document entirely.

    Concept Tested: Elevator Pitch See: Elevator Pitch

    2. How does a value proposition statement differ from a mission statement?¶

    1. A value proposition statement is written to persuade an outside audience, while a mission statement guides internal decisions
    2. A value proposition statement is only used for grant applications
    3. A mission statement is spoken aloud, while a value proposition statement is always written
    4. A value proposition statement replaces the need for a club charter
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter distinguishes the value proposition statement as written to persuade an outside audience like a skeptical parent, while the mission statement from Chapter 2 guides internal decisions about what the club does. Option B invents a grant-specific restriction not in the text. Option C reverses the actual distinction, which is about audience, not delivery format. Option D confuses the value proposition with the charter, an unrelated founding document.

    Concept Tested: Value Proposition Statement See: Value Proposition Statement

    3. What does club branding extend beyond the naming and logo choices from Chapter 4?¶

    1. A legal trademark registration filed with a state government
    2. A consistent visual and verbal identity -- the same colors, tone, and logo placement -- across every flyer, newsletter, and social post
    3. A fixed schedule of session times that never changes
    4. The club's insurance policy and liability coverage
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Club branding extends naming and logo choices into a consistent visual and verbal identity across communications, so families recognize club materials at a glance. Option A invents a legal filing requirement the chapter never discusses under branding. Option C confuses branding with scheduling, an unrelated logistics topic. Option D confuses branding with insurance, a safety and liability topic from earlier chapters.

    Concept Tested: Club Branding See: Club Branding

    4. How does scaling a club differ from multi-site expansion?¶

    1. Scaling requires a franchise agreement, while multi-site expansion does not
    2. Scaling and multi-site expansion are two names for the exact same process
    3. Scaling grows the existing single site with more students, sessions, and mentors, while multi-site expansion adds a second physical location
    4. Scaling only applies to school-based clubs, while multi-site expansion only applies to library clubs
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter defines scaling as growing the existing single site without adding a location, while multi-site expansion means running the same model at more than one venue, typically after the founding site has proven stable. Option A reverses the actual relationship -- a franchise model is a separate, later stage. Option B ignores the explicit distinction the chapter draws between the two terms. Option D invents venue-type restrictions the chapter never states.

    Concept Tested: Scaling A Club See: Scaling A Club

    5. What does onboarding new students specifically involve, according to the chapter?¶

    1. Requiring new students to complete a written entrance exam
    2. Pairing a new student with a returning student for peer support and giving them a slightly simplified version of the session's project
    3. Assigning every new student directly to the club leader for one-on-one instruction
    4. Delaying a new student's first hands-on project until their third session
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter describes onboarding new students as pairing them with a returning student for peer support and offering a simplified project so their first session ends in a completed build. Option A invents an entrance exam that contradicts the club's no-grades philosophy. Option C misassigns new students exclusively to the club leader, not the described peer-pairing approach. Option D contradicts the chapter's emphasis on a completed first-session build, not a delayed one.

    Concept Tested: Onboarding New Students See: Onboarding New Students

    6. Why does the chapter describe word of mouth referral as the highest-converting promotional channel even after a club's first meeting?¶

    1. Because it is the only channel that costs the club any money
    2. Because flyers and social media are no longer effective once a club has held its first meeting
    3. Because word of mouth referral is required before a club can accept new students
    4. Because a family hearing about the club from another family they already trust arrives with far less hesitation than one responding to a flyer
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter explains that a referral from an already-trusted family reduces hesitation more effectively than a flyer, which is why word of mouth remains the highest-converting channel long term. Option A is factually backwards -- word of mouth is typically free, unlike some media outreach. Option B overstates the case; the chapter never claims flyers or social media become useless. Option C invents a requirement not stated anywhere in the chapter.

    Concept Tested: Word Of Mouth Referral See: Word Of Mouth Referral

    7. A club's single site has grown steadily but has never yet gone through a change in who leads it. The founding leader is now considering opening a second location at a nearby school. What does the chapter's guidance suggest?¶

    1. Proceed with multi-site expansion immediately, since steady growth alone is sufficient justification
    2. Skip straight to a franchise model instead, since it requires less oversight than a second site
    3. The more urgent problem to solve first is proving the original site can survive a leadership transition, since a second site inherits every unsolved sustainability gap from the first
    4. Multi-site expansion should never be attempted by a club that started at a school
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's tip explicitly warns that a club considering multi-site expansion should first confirm its original site has survived a full leadership transition, since a second site inherits every unsolved sustainability gap from the first. Option A ignores this explicit precondition. Option B misapplies the franchise model, which is described as requiring even more independence, not less oversight. Option D invents a venue-type restriction the chapter never states.

    Concept Tested: Multi Site Expansion See: Multi Site Expansion

    8. A club leader wants to reach families through a trusted adult who already interacts with them regularly and can personally recommend the club while they check out books. Which outreach relationship does this describe?¶

    1. Press release writing
    2. School partnership outreach
    3. Library partnership outreach
    4. Club anniversary event
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter describes library partnership outreach as a children's librarian personally recommending the club to families checking out books, reaching exactly the families most likely to attend. Option A describes a media announcement tool, not a personal relationship. Option B describes a school-based relationship with administration or a teacher, a different venue context. Option D describes a milestone celebration, unrelated to ongoing outreach relationships.

    Concept Tested: Library Partnership Outreach See: Library Partnership Outreach

    9. Why does the chapter say a press release is most effective when tied to a specific newsworthy moment rather than sent as a generic "we exist" announcement?¶

    1. Because local outlets legally require a dateline on every submission
    2. Because a generic announcement uses a different headline format than a newsworthy one
    3. Because a press release without a newsworthy hook cannot include a quote from the club leader
    4. Because generic announcements are less likely to be picked up than ones with a concrete, dateable event or milestone to attach to
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter states a press release is most effective when tied to a specific newsworthy moment, and pairs this idea with milestone events like a grand opening, which give outreach something concrete to attach to. Option A invents a legal dateline requirement unrelated to newsworthiness. Option B misstates the actual issue, which is relevance, not formatting. Option C fabricates a restriction on including a quote that the chapter never mentions.

    Concept Tested: Press Release Writing See: Press Release Writing

    10. A club is celebrating its fifth year running, inviting alumni back and showcasing past student projects, which draws a second wave of local media interest. A different club down the street is instead hosting its very first public launch event with refreshments and a demonstration table for local officials. How do these two events differ?¶

    1. The fifth-year event is a club anniversary event, marking an ongoing club's milestone year, while the launch event is a grand opening event, marking a new club's or site's official debut
    2. Both events are examples of the same concept, a grand opening event, since both involve refreshments
    3. The fifth-year event is a first club meeting, while the launch event is a club anniversary event
    4. Neither event qualifies as a milestone event because neither was covered by local media coverage in advance
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter distinguishes a club anniversary event, which celebrates an ongoing club's first, fifth, or tenth year with alumni and past projects, from a grand opening event, which marks a new club's or site's official launch with extra visibility. Option B incorrectly merges two distinct concepts into one. Option C misapplies both terms to the wrong scenario. Option D invents a media-in-advance requirement the chapter never states as a qualifying condition.

    Concept Tested: Club Anniversary Event See: Club Anniversary Event
