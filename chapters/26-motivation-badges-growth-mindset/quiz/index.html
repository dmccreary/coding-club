    Quiz: Motivation, Badges, and Growth Mindset Coaching¶

    Test your understanding of the psychology behind badge and reward systems, careful competition design, and growth-mindset coaching language with these review questions.

    1. What is intrinsic motivation?¶

    1. A sticker awarded for finishing a challenge card
    2. A public ranking of students by total badges earned
    3. The drive to do something because the activity itself is satisfying, with no external reward required
    4. A certificate awarded for completing an entire track
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter defines intrinsic motivation as the drive to do something because the activity itself is satisfying -- curiosity, puzzle-solving pleasure, or pride in making something work -- with no external reward required, illustrated by a student staying late to fix a robot with nobody watching. Option A describes an extrinsic reward, the opposite kind of motivator. Option B describes a leaderboard, an unrelated competition tool. Option D describes a certificate of completion, another extrinsic reward.

    Concept Tested: Intrinsic Motivation See: Intrinsic Motivation

    2. What does good badge design require?¶

    1. Awarding a badge simply for attending a session
    2. Naming a badge after the specific skill it proves, using a distinct shape or color, and awarding it only after the skill is actually demonstrated rather than merely attempted
    3. Using the same generic "Great Job" label on every badge
    4. Reserving badges only for the fastest student in each session
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter's worked example shows a vague "Great Job" badge redesigned into specific badges like "Loop Detective," each awarded only when a mentor watches the student explain their own code, giving every badge a checkable meaning. Option A describes attendance-based rewards, the opposite of skill-demonstrated badges. Option C describes exactly the vague labeling the chapter's worked example moves away from. Option D invents a speed-based restriction the chapter never states for badges.

    Concept Tested: Badge Design See: Badge Design

    3. What is a certificate of completion?¶

    1. A small sticker placed on a card right at the moment of an accomplishment
    2. A shared wall poster showing the club's aggregate progress
    3. A single badge proving one specific skill
    4. A formal, printed document awarded when a student finishes an entire track, marking a milestone significant enough to hand to a parent and keep
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter defines a certificate of completion as a formal document marking an entire track finished, distinct from a badge's single-skill scope or a sticker's small in-session moment. Option A describes a sticker reward system, a smaller and more frequent reward. Option B describes a progress tracker poster, a collective rather than individual milestone marker. Option C describes a badge, which proves one skill rather than a whole track.

    Concept Tested: Certificate Of Completion See: Certificate Of Completion

    4. What does the chapter's mention of the "overjustification effect" warn a mentor about?¶

    1. Piling an extrinsic reward on top of a task a student already loves for its own sake can sometimes shrink their intrinsic motivation instead of adding to it
    2. Every extrinsic reward system is harmful and should never be used
    3. Certificates should only be awarded to the fastest student in each track
    4. Stickers are more motivating than badges for every age group
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter explains the overjustification effect using a "paying a friend for a favor" analogy: adding a reward on top of an already-loved task can quietly turn a joy into a job rather than simply adding value. Option B overstates the chapter's actual position, which describes extrinsic rewards as useful when designed carefully, not universally harmful. Option C invents a speed-based restriction never discussed for certificates. Option D makes an unsupported blanket comparison the chapter never states.

    Concept Tested: Extrinsic Reward System See: Extrinsic Reward System

    5. Why does the chapter treat leaderboard consideration as a real decision rather than a default choice?¶

    1. Because leaderboards are prohibited by the club's code of conduct
    2. Because a ranked list makes struggle visible and permanent in a way a private learning map never does, risking discouragement for students consistently ranked at the bottom
    3. Because leaderboards require a digital portfolio system to function
    4. Because leaderboards can only be used with students on the advanced track
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter's worked example shows two consistently bottom-ranked students attending less often after a badge leaderboard is posted, illustrating exactly this risk of visible, lasting discouragement. Option A invents a code-of-conduct prohibition the chapter never states. Option C fabricates a technical dependency between leaderboards and an unrelated portfolio tool. Option D invents a track restriction not discussed in the chapter.

    Concept Tested: Leaderboard Consideration See: Leaderboard Consideration

    6. How does friendly competition design differ from collaborative challenge design?¶

    1. Friendly competition design still involves teams competing against each other in a time-boxed, low-stakes format, while collaborative challenge design replaces competition entirely with one shared goal no single student can finish alone
    2. They are the same format described with two different names
    3. Collaborative challenge design always produces a public ranking, while friendly competition design never does
    4. Friendly competition design is reserved for advanced-track students only
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter distinguishes friendly competition design, which keeps team-based, time-boxed competing with humor and re-paired rounds, from collaborative challenge design, which removes competition entirely in favor of one shared outcome requiring everyone's contribution. Option B ignores the chapter's explicit distinction between the two formats. Option C reverses the actual ranking relationship, since collaborative design has no ranking at all. Option D invents a track restriction the chapter never states for either format.

    Concept Tested: Collaborative Challenge Design See: Collaborative Challenge Design

    7. A student whose robot keeps crashing says "I just can't do this." The mentor responds, "You can't do it yet -- last month you couldn't get the LED strip to blink, and now you can program five patterns. What's one thing you could try differently?" Which concept does this response illustrate?¶

    1. Public recognition event
    2. Leaderboard consideration
    3. Sticker reward system
    4. Growth mindset coaching, which frames ability as something that grows with effort and practice rather than a fixed trait, often through adding the word "yet"
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter's own worked example uses this exact "yet" language and points back to a past struggle the student already overcame, the core technique of growth mindset coaching. Option A describes a larger, scheduled public event, not an in-the-moment coaching response. Option B describes a ranking system, unrelated to coaching language. Option C describes a small physical reward, not a verbal coaching response.

    Concept Tested: Growth Mindset Coaching See: Growth Mindset Coaching

    8. A club initially gives a sticker for every single line of code a student writes, and within two sessions students collect stickers without even looking at them. What fix does the chapter's worked example recommend?¶

    1. Switch entirely to a leaderboard instead of stickers
    2. Give every student a certificate of completion instead
    3. Reserving stickers for a card actually completed, rather than every small step along the way, to restore the moment of genuine excitement each sticker is meant to produce
    4. Stop using any extrinsic rewards for the rest of the semester
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's worked example diagnoses the problem as over-frequent awarding and fixes it by reserving stickers for actual card completions, restoring their meaning rather than eliminating them. Option A substitutes a different reward system entirely, not the fix the chapter describes. Option B jumps to an unrelated, much larger reward inappropriate for this small-scale problem. Option D is a more extreme response than the targeted frequency fix the chapter actually recommends.

    Concept Tested: Sticker Reward System See: Sticker Reward System

    9. A mentor runs a fifteen-minute "fastest LED blink" contest scored by pairs rather than individuals, with losing pairs re-paired into new teams for a second round so no student stays associated with a loss for more than fifteen minutes. Which concept does this format illustrate?¶

    1. Certificate of completion
    2. Learning map display
    3. Friendly competition design, built to be low-stakes and short-lived on purpose, scored by team and framed with humor
    4. Progress tracker poster
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter's own worked example describes exactly this contest: time-boxed, team-scored, with re-pairing between rounds so no single student's name stays attached to a loss, the defining features of friendly competition design. Option A describes an unrelated track-completion document. Option B describes a personal progress display, unrelated to a timed contest. Option D describes a shared club-wide progress display, not a competitive activity.

    Concept Tested: Friendly Competition Design See: Friendly Competition Design

    10. A club posts a badge leaderboard, and the two students consistently ranked last stop attending as often, saying the board makes them feel "bad at this." According to the chapter's overall framing, what combination of concepts addresses this situation going forward?¶

    1. Adding more badge tiers to the same leaderboard so more students rank near the top
    2. Removing the shared ranking in favor of each student's own progress shown privately, paired with growth-mindset coaching language and small-win celebration to keep struggling students engaged
    3. Switching immediately to a certificate of completion for every student regardless of progress
    4. Increasing the frequency of stickers awarded to the two struggling students specifically
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter's own worked example resolves this exact leaderboard problem by replacing the shared ranking with a private view of each student's own badge count over time, and the chapter's broader framing pairs that fix with growth-mindset coaching and celebrating small wins to keep struggling students engaged without public comparison. Option A keeps the harmful ranking structure intact rather than removing it. Option C mismatches the reward scale, since a certificate marks an entire track, not a response to a leaderboard problem. Option D singles out the two students in a way that would make their struggle even more visible, the opposite of the chapter's actual fix.

    Concept Tested: Leaderboard Consideration See: Leaderboard Consideration
