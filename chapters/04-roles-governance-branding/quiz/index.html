    Quiz: Club Roles, Governance Structure, and Branding¶

    Test your understanding of the people roles a coding club needs, its governance structure, safety logistics, and branding basics with these review questions.

    1. What does the mentor role cover in a coding club?¶

    1. Any adult who has passed a background check and works directly with students during sessions
    2. Any adult who drops off and picks up a student
    3. The person who tracks the club's annual budget exclusively
    4. A student who has attended more than ten sessions
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The mentor role covers any adult who has passed a background check and works directly with students, guiding projects and applying the child safety policy during sessions. Option B describes the parent role instead. Option C narrows mentoring to budget tracking, which the chapter never claims. Option D confuses a mentor with an experienced peer student, a different relationship entirely.

    2. What is the volunteer coordinator role primarily responsible for?¶

    1. Teaching every session's curriculum directly to students
    2. Designing the club's logo and promotional flyers
    3. Signing the venue agreement on behalf of the club
    4. Recruiting new mentors, tracking background check status, and scheduling mentor coverage
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter describes the volunteer coordinator as recruiting new mentors, tracking who has completed a background check and when it expires, and scheduling mentor coverage for each session. Option A describes the mentor role's session-time responsibility. Option B describes a branding task not assigned to this role. Option C describes a club leader responsibility, not the coordinator's.

    3. What is a governance structure, as the chapter defines it?¶

    1. A legal filing required before a club can accept donations
    2. The overall arrangement of roles, oversight, and reporting lines that determines who is accountable to whom
    3. The physical layout of tables and equipment in a session room
    4. A fixed curriculum sequence every mentor must follow in order
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Governance structure is defined as the overall arrangement of roles, oversight, and reporting lines determining accountability, illustrated by the chapter's hierarchical diagram connecting advisory committee, leaders, coordinator, mentors, students, and parents. Option A describes a legal compliance matter, a separate topic. Option C describes classroom layout, unrelated to governance. Option D describes curriculum planning, not a reporting structure.

    Concept Tested: Governance Structure See: Governance Structure

    4. According to the chapter, when does a club typically add an advisory committee?¶

    1. Before the club's very first session, regardless of size
    2. Only when a state law explicitly mandates one
    3. Once the club grows large enough that a single leader's judgment alone starts to feel like a risk, or once it handles a meaningful budget
    4. Only after a serious safety incident has already occurred
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter states the general rule of thumb is adding an advisory committee once a single leader's judgment alone starts to feel risky, or once the club manages a budget that benefits from more oversight, as the fifty-student example illustrates. Option A contradicts the chapter's point that tiny clubs run fine without one. Option B invents a legal mandate not discussed. Option D wrongly ties the decision to an incident rather than growth or budget size.

    5. How does the club policies handbook differ from the club charter?¶

    1. The handbook is a legal filing while the charter is purely informal
    2. The handbook is the practical, frequently-updated companion covering procedures, while the charter states foundational rules changed rarely and deliberately
    3. The handbook only covers mentor pay, while the charter covers everything else
    4. The handbook is written by parents, while the charter is written by students
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter contrasts the charter's foundational, rarely-changed rules with the handbook's practical, frequently-updated procedures, such as how to submit a background check or where supplies are tracked. Option A reverses the actual formality relationship between the two documents. Option C narrows the handbook to a topic the chapter never restricts it to. Option D invents authorship roles not discussed in the text.

    6. A student trips over a loose cable and scrapes a knee during a session. Which process should the mentor use to document what happened, and how does it differ from the conflict resolution process in Chapter 3?¶

    1. The conflict resolution process, since any negative event at a session counts as a conflict
    2. The venue agreement, since injuries are always the venue's legal responsibility
    3. The club policies handbook, since it lists every possible injury scenario in advance
    4. The incident reporting process, which documents physical injuries and safety incidents, distinct from the conflict resolution process that handles interpersonal disputes
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter explicitly distinguishes the incident reporting process, used for physical injuries or safety incidents, from the conflict resolution process, which handles interpersonal disputes rather than injuries. Option A misapplies the interpersonal-dispute process to a physical injury. Option B wrongly assumes the venue automatically bears legal responsibility. Option C misdescribes the handbook as an exhaustive injury catalog, which it is not.

    1. Insurance considerations and the venue agreement
    2. Club naming convention and trademark and branding
    3. Advisory committee and club policies handbook
    4. Student role and parent role
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The chapter states many venues require a certificate of insurance naming the venue as an additional insured before signing a venue agreement, directly linking insurance considerations to the formal venue agreement. Option B describes unrelated branding concepts. Option C describes oversight and documentation roles not tied to insurance requirements. Option D describes participant roles, unrelated to insurance or venue paperwork.

    8. A club keeps its only copy of the emergency contact list saved in the club leader's personal phone. What problem does the chapter say this creates?¶

    1. It violates the club's trademark and branding guidelines
    2. It has no real downside as long as the leader keeps her phone charged
    3. It recreates the single leader dependency problem in miniature, this time with safety-critical consequences
    4. It automatically voids the club's venue agreement
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The chapter warns that keeping the emergency contact list only in one mentor's phone recreates single leader dependency in miniature, but with safety-critical stakes if that phone is unavailable during an emergency. Option A confuses this with an unrelated branding issue. Option B dismisses a risk the chapter explicitly flags. Option D invents a venue-agreement consequence not stated in the text.

    9. Why does the chapter warn that a venue contact saying "yeah, just come by Tuesdays" is not sufficient to start a club?¶

    1. Because state law requires every agreement to be notarized before minors can attend
    2. Because verbal arrangements are the first thing that falls apart when the venue contact changes jobs, unlike a written venue agreement covering times, fees, and insurance
    3. Because a verbal arrangement automatically transfers legal liability to the club leader personally
    4. Because Tuesdays are historically the least reliable day for after-school programs
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The chapter warns that verbal arrangements are the first thing to fall apart when a venue contact changes jobs, so meeting times, insurance responsibility, and cancellation policy need to be in writing before telling families the club is happening. Option A invents a notarization requirement not discussed. Option C fabricates an automatic liability transfer the chapter never claims. Option D is an irrelevant, unsupported claim about weekday reliability.
