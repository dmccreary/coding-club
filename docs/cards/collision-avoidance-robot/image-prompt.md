# Collision-Avoidance Robot Image Prompt

## Subject

The illustration must depict one small two-wheeled floor robot, built from
a flat SmartCar-style chassis with a control board mounted on top and a
forward-facing distance sensor, shown actively detecting an obstacle
ahead of it. The scene needs exactly three things: the robot, one
obstacle (a simple rectangular block, not a wall filling the frame), and
a visible "sensing beam" connecting the sensor to the obstacle so a
viewer immediately understands the robot is measuring distance, not just
driving. No second robot, no additional obstacles, no background clutter.

## Reference Image

Use the following image as a reference:
![Collision Avoidance Robot Using the Cytron Maker Pi RP2040 Board](./maker-pi-rp2040-robot.jpg)

## Composition

Wide landscape canvas, 1774 x 887 pixels (2:1 aspect ratio), clean white
background, no horizon line, no tabletop edge, no room or wall behind the
scene. View the robot from a three-quarter angle from above and slightly
to the side, the same vantage point used in `motor-spin.png`, so the
chassis, wheels, and top-mounted board are all readable at once. Place
the robot in the left half of the frame, facing right. Place the
rectangular obstacle block in the right third of the frame, with enough
white space between robot and obstacle for the sensing beam to read
clearly. Every element must stay fully inside the frame with margin on
all sides.

## The robot

The chassis is a flat, rounded-rectangle clear or light-gray acrylic
platform, seen at a three-quarter angle, with two large black rubber
wheels with visible tire tread, one mounted near the front-left and one
near the front-right of the chassis, each turned by a small yellow
gearmotor visible at the hub. A small unpowered caster or skid shows at
the rear of the chassis for balance, drawn simply, not a focal point.

Mounted on top of the chassis sits a compact rectangular circuit board in
deep purple or dark navy, with a scattering of small silver-colored
surface-mount components, a row of round LED indicator dots along one
edge, and a small USB connector visible on one side. The board sits flat
and centered on the chassis, proportioned so the chassis is clearly the
larger structure underneath it.

At the very front of the chassis, facing the direction of travel, sits a
small sensor module: a compact black rectangular housing with one or two
small circular lens elements on its front face, mounted on a short
bracket so it points forward and slightly outward toward the obstacle.

## The sensing beam and obstacle

From the sensor's lens, draw a narrow, semi-transparent triangular or
cone-shaped beam in sky blue (`#4A90D9`), widening slightly as it travels
right and terminating at the face of the obstacle block. The beam should
read as a measurement/detection cone, not a solid laser line -- soft
edges, mostly transparent, so the white background shows through it.

The obstacle is a simple rectangular block (like a painted wood block or
foam cube), solid warm amber (`#F5A623`), with a flat front face directly
facing the robot and simple flat shading on its visible top and side
faces to give it a light three-dimensional presence. It has no texture,
pattern, or label -- a plain colored block, clearly an obstacle and
nothing more specific (not a wall, not a piece of furniture, not another
robot).

## Style, color, and readability

Render as a polished educational illustration with bold, confident dark
outlines and smooth flat-to-soft-gradient shading, matching the style of
`motor-spin.png` and `rock-paper-scissors.png`. Primary palette: warm
amber (`#F5A623`) for the obstacle, sky blue (`#4A90D9`) for the sensing
beam, black and dark gray for wheels and the sensor housing, deep
purple/navy for the control board, and light gray/white for the chassis
-- all on a white background. Keep silhouettes simple and high-contrast
so the robot, its sensor, the beam, and the obstacle are all still
distinguishable when the card is printed small and laminated.

## Avoid

No text, labels, numbers, distance readouts, or measurement marks. No
people, hands, faces, or mascot characters. No logos, watermarks, or
brand names on the board or chassis. No photorealism -- flat/vector
illustration only. No second robot, no wall or room background, no extra
props (no laptop, no cables snaking off-frame, no tools). Do not make the
sensing beam look like a solid laser line or a lightning bolt.
