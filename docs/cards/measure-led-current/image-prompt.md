# Image Prompt: Measure the Current of a Red LED

Please generate an illustration for the front of this challenge card, showing
a red LED circuit with its current marked as an unknown to be solved for.

**Required specifications:**
- Format: PNG, transparent or white background
- Square format, 1000x1000 pixels
- Flat vector illustration style, clean lines, bold simple shapes — matching
  this book's mascot and diagram art style, not a photorealistic rendering

## Subject

A small breadboard circuit, drawn simply and clearly:

- A red LED, lit and glowing (a soft radial glow behind the bulb), mounted
  in a breadboard.
- A resistor (tan body with color bands) wired in series next to the LED.
- Two or three simple jumper wires connecting the LED and resistor across
  the breadboard, suggesting a complete series loop without drawing a full
  battery or power supply in detail.
- Along the wire between the resistor and the LED, place a bold italic
  capital **"I"** (the standard symbol for electrical current) inside or
  next to a small circle or arrow showing the direction of current flow.
- Immediately beside that "I", place a large, bold **question mark ("?")**
  — noticeably bigger than the "I" — making clear that the amount of
  current is the unknown the student must calculate and then measure.

## Style & Composition

- Color palette: warm amber (`#F5A623`) for the glowing LED and the "I" /
  "?" marks, sky blue (`#4A90D9`) for wires or accent details, on a clean
  white or transparent background — matching this book's mascot color
  palette.
- Composition: the circuit sits in the lower two-thirds of the image; the
  "I" and oversized "?" are positioned in the open space just above the
  wire between the resistor and LED, large enough to read clearly even
  at a small printed size (the card's front panel icon area is only a
  few inches wide).
- Keep the whole illustration simple and uncluttered — this is a small
  card icon, not a detailed technical schematic.

## Avoid

- Do not render any other text, labels, numbers, or component names in the
  image — only the "I" and "?" marks.
- Do not draw a battery, USB cable, or multimeter — the challenge is about
  the LED/resistor pair only.
- Avoid photorealistic rendering, shadows, or textures — keep it flat and
  vector-styled.
