# References: Telling Your Club's Story and Designing the Classroom

1. [Library makerspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_makerspace) - Wikipedia - History and design goals of library-hosted maker and coding spaces, directly relevant to this chapter's room-layout guidance for a club meeting in a shared community space.

2. [Newsletter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsletter) - Wikipedia - Overview of the newsletter format and its use by organizations to keep an audience informed, the communication channel this chapter recommends for sharing club milestones.

3. [Testimonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testimonial) - Wikipedia - Explains how organizations collect and use first-person endorsements, the technique behind this chapter's testimonial-collection guidance.

4. Blueprint for Tomorrow: Redesigning Schools for Student-Centered Learning - Prakash Nair - Harvard Education Press - Nair pioneered evidence-based flexible classroom design, including sightlines and cluster seating for collaborative supervision, the reasoning behind this chapter's monitors-facing-inward and center-table layout.

5. Made to Stick: Why Some Ideas Survive and Others Die - Chip Heath and Dan Heath - Random House - The Heath brothers are credited with the "SUCCESS" framework (Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Story) for crafting memorable messages, the storytelling technique behind this chapter's success-story and testimonial guidance.

6. [Makerspace Design Guide](https://k12maker.mit.edu/design-guide.html) - MIT Fab Foundation / K12 Maker Program - Detailed square-footage, furniture, storage, and safety guidance for setting up a school or club makerspace, directly supporting this chapter's classroom-layout section.

7. [A Flexible Classroom Seating Design](https://www.edutopia.org/article/flexible-classroom-seating-design/) - Edutopia - Explains the L-shaped desk arrangement that lets students face the front while collaborating in small groups, a concrete model for this chapter's center-table and seating guidance.

8. [How to Write the Best Email Newsletter](https://mailchimp.com/resources/email/email-newsletter-examples/) - Mailchimp - Practical guidance on subject lines, content mix, and format for a recurring newsletter, supporting this chapter's club-newsletter section.

9. [Girls Who Code | Start a Club](https://girlswhocode.com/get-involved/start-a-club) - Girls Who Code - Shows how an established club network presents its story and milestones publicly, a model for the success-story sharing this chapter recommends.

10. [Power strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_strip) - Wikipedia - Covers overload risk, daisy-chaining restrictions, and fire-code rules for power strips, the safety background behind this chapter's power-strip-placement and cable-management guidance.
