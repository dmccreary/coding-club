# Quiz: Tracking Expenses and Building Funding Partnerships

Test your understanding of expense reporting, reimbursement, donation receipts, annual budget review, and the year-over-year funding partnerships that keep a no-fee club funded past its first year with these review questions.

#### 1. What is a budget tracking spreadsheet?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A one-page document used to pitch a school district or PTA
2. The forecast document created before a club's year begins
3. A running, dated record of every dollar a club actually receives or spends, kept separate from the budget so a leader can compare what was planned against what really happened
4. A written policy stating what expenses a club will reimburse
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The chapter distinguishes the tracking spreadsheet, a ledger of what actually happened, from the budget itself, a forecast made in advance -- the two are meant to be compared side by side. Option A describes a partnership proposal template, an unrelated pitching document. Option B describes the budget from Chapter 30, the forecast the spreadsheet is compared against, not the spreadsheet itself. Option D describes a reimbursement policy, a different financial document.

    **Concept Tested:** Budget Tracking Spreadsheet
    **See:** [Budget Tracking Spreadsheet](../../glossary.md#budget-tracking-spreadsheet)

#### 2. How does an emergency fund reserve differ from equipment depreciation planning?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. An emergency fund reserve is saved for a known, expected replacement, while equipment depreciation planning is saved for the unknown
2. An emergency fund reserve is saved for an unplanned, unpredictable cost, while equipment depreciation planning sets aside a specific amount for equipment's known, expected eventual replacement
3. They are the same reserve, just described using two different terms
4. Equipment depreciation planning only applies to laptops, never to kits or furniture
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. The chapter explicitly distinguishes the two: an emergency fund reserve covers an unplanned cost like a laptop dying mid-semester, while equipment depreciation planning sets aside a known, calculated amount for equipment's expected eventual replacement. Option A reverses which reserve covers the known versus the unknown cost. Option C ignores the chapter's explicit distinction between the two reserves. Option D contradicts the chapter's own depreciation table, which covers Chromebooks, kits, and a storage cart together.

    **Concept Tested:** Emergency Fund Reserve
    **See:** [Emergency Fund Reserve](../../glossary.md#emergency-fund-reserve)

</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. This combination draws directly on the chapter's own institutional partnership examples -- district in-kind support, a library's cash and space allocation -- paired with the sliding scale model the chapter presents as the middle path that avoids the flat fee's exclusion risk, together avoiding dependence on any single source. Option A reintroduces the flat-fee exclusion risk the chapter explicitly warns against. Option C recreates the single-point-of-failure risk the chapter's diversified funding examples are built to avoid. Option D ignores the chapter's institutional partnerships entirely in favor of the least predictable funding source described.

    **Concept Tested:** Sliding Scale Fee Model
