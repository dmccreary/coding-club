## Special Event Formats

Regular weekly or biweekly sessions form a club's backbone, but eight distinct special event formats give it variety and reach beyond that routine. A **weeklong intensive format** compresses a term's worth of a single topic into consecutive daily sessions, similar in structure to the summer camp format from Chapter 12 but usable any time school is out. A **single-session workshop** is the opposite extreme: one standalone session, often open to non-members, focused on a single skill -- a two-hour "build your first LED badge" workshop that doubles as informal recruiting for the ongoing club. **Field trip coordination** takes students off-site entirely, to a local tech company or a robotics competition, and requires its own consent paperwork layered on top of the standard parental consent form from Chapter 3.

**Guest speaker scheduling** brings in an outside professional for a single session -- a software engineer, a game designer -- to talk about their work and answer questions, most valuable when scheduled to coincide with a topic the current curriculum track is already covering. **Competition event scheduling** organizes participation in an external contest or hackathon, requiring careful coordination with that competition's own registration deadlines and rules. A **showcase event** is the club's own end-of-term exhibition, where students present finished projects to family and community members -- the single highest-visibility event most clubs run all year. A **family night event** invites parents and siblings to build alongside students for one session, and an **open house event** invites the broader public in to see what the club does, often timed alongside a grand opening or anniversary event from Chapter 6.

The table below summarizes all eight formats together.

| Event Format | Typical Length | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Weeklong Intensive Format | 4-5 consecutive days | Deep focus on one topic during a school break |
| Single Session Workshop | One session, 1-3 hours | Recruiting new members, low-commitment trial |
| Field Trip Coordination | Half-day to full-day, off-site | Exposure to real workplaces or competitions |
| Guest Speaker Scheduling | Part of one regular session | Career exposure tied to current curriculum |

| Showcase Event Planning | One session, end of term | Visible proof of what students built |

Pick the format that matches the specific gap you're trying to close, not the one that sounds most exciting.

## Payments and Access

Most of this book assumes a no-fee model (Chapter 30 covers why), but some clubs, especially for special events like a weeklong intensive, do charge. A **registration software comparison** weighs platforms like Eventbrite (Chapter 11) against alternatives on cost, ease of setup, and whether it natively supports mentor-gated capacity limits. Where fees apply, **payment processing for fees** covers how money actually changes hands -- typically a built-in payment processor inside the registration platform rather than cash handled in person, which avoids both bookkeeping headaches and safety concerns around minors handling money.

A **scholarship application process** ensures fees never become the reason a student can't attend: a simple, low-friction form -- ideally requiring no proof of need beyond a parent's request -- that waives or reduces a fee, kept as private as possible so a scholarship student never feels singled out among their peers.

!!! mascot-tip "Make the scholarship ask as easy as the registration itself"
    ![Circuit giving a tip](../../img/mascot/tip.png){ class="mascot-admonition-img" }
    Here's a shortcut: put a one-line scholarship link directly next to the payment field on your registration form, not buried on a separate page. A family who has to hunt for a financial-aid option is a family who may quietly decide not to register at all.

## Notifications and Logistics

A **session reminder notification** -- typically a text or push notification the morning of a session -- catches families who might miss an email reminder, especially valuable for special events happening outside the normal recurring rhythm. **Calendar sync for families** lets a family add the club's full schedule directly to their own phone or household calendar with one click, reducing the odds of a forgotten session far more effectively than any number of individual reminders. For a club serving a spread-out or partially remote community, **time zone consideration** matters for anything involving a virtual guest speaker or a registration deadline tied to a specific time -- always state the time zone explicitly rather than assuming everyone shares the venue's local time.

## Weather, Makeups, and Wrapping Up the Term

A clear **weather cancellation policy** states in advance how a decision to cancel for snow or severe weather gets made and communicated -- who makes the call, by what time, and through which channel -- so families aren't left guessing on a stormy morning. A **makeup session policy** decides what happens to a canceled session's content: is it simply skipped, folded into the next session, or rescheduled as a standalone add-on, decided ahead of time rather than improvised the day a cancellation happens.

Every term should close deliberately with an **end of term celebration** -- which might be the showcase event described earlier, or a simpler, lower-key gathering -- giving students a clear sense of accomplishment and giving the club a natural, positive close before starting the next term's onboarding fresh.

!!! mascot-warning "Decide your weather policy before the first storm, not during it"
    ![Circuit warning](../../img/mascot/warning.png){ class="mascot-admonition-img" }
    Watch out for this: trying to decide a cancellation policy in the middle of an actual snowstorm, with families already texting to ask, produces a rushed and inconsistent call. Write the weather cancellation policy and makeup session policy into your club policies handbook (Chapter 4) before you ever need them.
