# References: Building Mentor-Student Relationships That Last

1. [Volunteering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteering) - Wikipedia - Overview of what motivates and sustains volunteer participation, the backdrop for this chapter's mentor-recruitment, appreciation, and retention guidance.

2. [Occupational burnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_burnout) - Wikipedia - Explains the causes and warning signs of burnout, directly relevant to this chapter's mentor-burnout-prevention section.

3. [Experience Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experience_Corps) - Wikipedia - Describes a national program that trains adults over 50 as literacy tutors, a real precedent for this chapter's retired-professional-mentor category and its one-to-one and small-group formats.

4. The Volunteer Recruitment (and Membership Development) Book (4th Edition) - Susan J. Ellis - Energize, Inc. - Ellis is widely regarded as the field's leading authority on volunteer management, including the recognition and burnout-prevention practices this chapter's mentor-appreciation section draws on.

5. Stand By Me: The Risks and Rewards of Mentoring Today's Youth - Jean E. Rhodes - Harvard University Press - Rhodes's research identified relationship consistency and closeness as the strongest predictors of a mentoring relationship's success, the evidence behind this chapter's emphasis on mentor-pairing consistency.

6. [Volunteer Retention](https://www.energizeinc.com/art/volunteer-retention) - Susan J. Ellis, Energize Inc. - Reframes volunteer retention around meaningful work, recognition, and a positive environment, supporting this chapter's mentor check-in, feedback, and appreciation guidance.

7. [What Makes an Effective College Student Volunteer Mentor?](https://www.evidencebasedmentoring.org/what-makes-an-effective-college-student-volunteer-mentor/) - The Chronicle of Evidence-Based Mentoring - Summarizes research on the traits of successful college-age mentors, supporting this chapter's college-mentor-recruitment section.

8. [Elements of Effective Practice for Mentoring](https://eepm.mentoring.org/) - MENTOR: National Mentoring Partnership - The field's standard framework for building and sustaining mentoring relationships, including attendance tracking and substitute planning, supporting several of this chapter's operational routines.

9. [Programs](https://www.bbbs.org/programs/) - Big Brothers Big Sisters of America - Illustrates one-to-one, small-group, and school-based mentoring formats in practice, the same formats this chapter compares for a coding club.

10. [Start a Club](https://girlswhocode.com/get-involved/start-a-club) - Girls Who Code - Documents how a national club network recruits teen, college, and adult volunteers, a real-world reference point for this chapter's mentor-recruitment-source guidance.
