# References: Sensors, Displays, Motors, and Robot Chassis

1. [Sensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensor) - Wikipedia - Explains how sensors convert physical phenomena like light, temperature, and motion into electrical signals, the shared principle behind every sensor this chapter covers.

2. [Servomotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servomotor) - Wikipedia - Explains how feedback control lets a servo motor hold a precise position, distinguishing it from the DC and stepper motors this chapter compares.

3. [Schematic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schematic) - Wikipedia - Covers how standardized symbols represent components and connections, the skill behind this chapter's circuit-diagram-reading section.

4. Encyclopedia of Electronic Components, Volume 1 - Charles Platt and Fredrik Jansson - Maker Media - Platt's component-by-component reference is the standard beginner guide to identifying and choosing sensors, motors, and displays, the same catalog approach this chapter takes.

5. Robot Building for Beginners (3rd Edition) - David Cook - Apress - Cook is widely recommended as the clearest introduction to choosing a robot chassis and matching motors and sensors to a first robot build, the guidance this chapter's robot-chassis section follows.

6. [Sensors](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/tags/sensors?page=all) - SparkFun Learn - A tagged collection of hookup guides for light, temperature, humidity, motion, sound, and distance sensors, directly supporting this chapter's sensor-basics survey.

7. [Monochrome OLED Breakouts](https://learn.adafruit.com/monochrome-oled-breakouts) - Adafruit Learning System - Explains how an OLED display's driver chip and wiring work, supporting this chapter's display-basics and OLED-display sections.

8. [Hobby Servo Tutorial](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/hobby-servo-tutorial/all) - SparkFun Learn - Explains how a servo's feedback control differs from a plain DC motor, directly supporting this chapter's motor-basics and servo-motor sections.

9. [Circuit 5A: Motor Basics](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/sparkfun-inventors-kit-experiment-guide---v41/circuit-5a-motor-basics) - SparkFun Learn - Explains why a DC motor needs a driver board rather than a direct digital-pin connection, directly supporting this chapter's DC-motor and motor-basics sections.

10. [Mobile robot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_robot) - Wikipedia - Surveys wheeled, legged, and tracked locomotion methods, the classification behind this chapter's robot-chassis and floor-robot sections.
