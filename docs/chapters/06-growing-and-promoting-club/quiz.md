# Quiz: Growing, Branding, and Promoting Your Club

Test your understanding of crafting a pitch, deciding whether to scale, building a brand, and promoting a club through partnerships, media, and milestone events with these review questions.

---

#### 1. What is an elevator pitch?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A written grant proposal submitted to a local foundation
2. A thirty-second spoken summary of what the club does and why it matters
3. The club's full annual budget presented to an advisory committee
4. A signed agreement between the club and a venue
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. The chapter defines an elevator pitch as a thirty-second spoken summary answering who the club serves, what students do, and why it's different, named for the time it takes an elevator to reach its floor. Option A describes a grant proposal, a written document for funders. Option C describes a budget presentation, unrelated to a spoken pitch. Option D describes a venue agreement, a different legal document entirely.

#### 4. How does scaling a club differ from multi-site expansion?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Scaling requires a franchise agreement, while multi-site expansion does not
2. Scaling and multi-site expansion are two names for the exact same process
3. Scaling grows the existing single site with more students, sessions, and mentors, while multi-site expansion adds a second physical location
4. Scaling only applies to school-based clubs, while multi-site expansion only applies to library clubs
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The chapter defines scaling as growing the existing single site without adding a location, while multi-site expansion means running the same model at more than one venue, typically after the founding site has proven stable. Option A reverses the actual relationship -- a franchise model is a separate, later stage. Option B ignores the explicit distinction the chapter draws between the two terms. Option D invents venue-type restrictions the chapter never states.

#### 10. A club is celebrating its fifth year running, inviting alumni back and showcasing past student projects, which draws a second wave of local media interest. <div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The fifth-year event is a club anniversary event, marking an ongoing club's milestone year, while the launch event is a grand opening event, marking a new club's or site's official debut
2. Both events are examples of the same concept, a grand opening event, since both involve refreshments
3. The fifth-year event is a first club meeting, while the launch event is a club anniversary event
4. Neither event qualifies as a milestone event because neither was covered by local media coverage in advance
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The chapter distinguishes a club anniversary event, which celebrates an ongoing club's first, fifth, or tenth year with alumni and past projects, from a grand opening event, which marks a new club's or site's official launch with extra visibility. Option B incorrectly merges two distinct concepts into one. Option C misapplies both terms to the wrong scenario. Option D invents a media-in-advance requirement the chapter never states as a qualifying condition.

    **Concept Tested:** Club Anniversary Event
    **See:** [Club Anniversary Event](../../glossary.md#club-anniversary-event)
