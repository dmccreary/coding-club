# Mini-Graphic Novel Story Ideas

## The Broken Wires on the Motors

Dan was a new volunteer for a large coding club that met on a local university campus.  Every week there were over 100 students that attended the club and there were almost 40 mentors.  However, the Raspberry Pi Pico kits that the club owned were severely disorganized and neglected.  Each of the kits should have had many parts in it: the microcontroller, the breadboard, hookup wires, LEDs, resistors, sensors, motors and displays.  But at the end of each session the students often just threw random parts in each kit from the tables.  Some kits had no parts and some had duplicate parts.

What Dan found most disturbing was that almost all the motors had their wires broken off.
He saw that although students enjoyed the labs, there were parts that were very frustrating!
This meant that students had to search through all the kits to find a working motor.
Dan decided the students deserved better.
He took the motors home re-soldered the wires on the motors.
Then he added heat-sensitive shrink wrap around the motor connections.
Next he use cable-ties to fasten the wires to the motors so they did not come lose.
Finally he added a small golden spiral to the motors axel so that students could see what direction they were spinning.  Learning how to reverse motors was one of the key learning objectives for the next robotics course.
Every week Dan watch the students use the kits and made careful note about how the kits could be improved.
Within a year he had restocked the missing parts, added new lessons and created a online textbook that went with the kits.  These changes didn't happen all at once.  Each week the notes make the kits a little better and the students had more fun.  They then told their friends about the fun they had with the kits and brought their friends.  Some students even purchased their own kits and brought them to the coding clubs.  The club slowly grew and expanded.

The moral of the story is that by careful observation, volunteers and mentors can make coding clubs a lot more fun!
