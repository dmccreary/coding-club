# Coding Club Interest Survey: Sample Employer Letter

This is a sample letter and survey that a company can send to its employees to
gauge interest in hosting a coding club. This version is written for an
organization with about 1,500 employees, shown as **ORGANIZATION**. Only about 300 of them
live in Minnesota near the Plymouth headquarters. The letter therefore explains the
video-link option for employees at other sites.

Replace the bracketed items before sending.

---

**To:** All ORGANIZATION Employees

**Date:** [Date]  
**Subject:** Would your child like to join an ORGANIZATION Coding Club?

Dear ORGANIZATION Colleagues,

We are exploring a **ORGANIZATION Coding Club** for students ages 10 to 16, and we
would like to know whether you, or someone in your family, would be interested.
This takes about two minutes to answer. Nothing you say here commits you to anything.

## What Is a Coding Club?

A coding club is a place where kids learn to write programs by building things they
can see, touch, and show to their friends. Students don't sit through lectures. They
make a program that draws a picture, or a clock that sits on their desk, and then
they figure out how to make it better.

Here is how the club would work.

- **Where and when:** The club would meet **once a month** at the ORGANIZATION
  headquarters in Plymouth, Minnesota.
- **Who:** Students ages **10 to 16**. No coding experience is needed.
- **Join by video:** We can provide a **video link** so employees at other ORGANIZATION
  sites can join each meeting remotely.
- **Show and tell:** Each quarter the club holds a show and tell where students
  present their own projects to the group, their families, and their coworkers.

## Two Ways to Learn

Every student can choose the format that fits them, or do both.

### 1. Online, at your own pace: Python with turtle graphics

Students learn Python by telling a "turtle" how to move around the screen and draw
shapes, patterns, and art. The lessons are completely online, so students can work
at their own speed at home. They can come to the monthly meeting to share what they
made and get help with anything that is stuck.

### 2. In person, with hands-on kits: MicroPython

At the monthly meetings, students build real electronics projects using small kits
and write MicroPython code that runs on the hardware. Over **60 different kits** are
available to choose from.

For example, the **$20 SmartClock kit** lets a student build their own clock. It
shows the time and weather, and has a stopwatch and a timer. It can even show
notifications about upcoming school events.

[See the SmartClock kit](https://dmccreary.github.io/clocks-and-watches/kits/sw-gc9b72/)

Families who want to take a kit home can purchase it. Kits cost **$20 to $50**,
depending on the cost of the parts.
